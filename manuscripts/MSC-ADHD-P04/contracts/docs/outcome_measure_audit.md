# Primary outcome measurement audit

Status: pre-freeze, updated 2026-09-28.

The primary external outcome for the future confirmatory ARC-ADHD trial is intentionally not selected yet. This document records the current candidate audit and the evidence required before the endpoint can be frozen.

## Decision criteria

A candidate must be assessed for:

1. construct correspondence with the ARC-ADHD far-transfer claim;
2. suitability for children aged 8 to 12 years;
3. Polish-language availability and independent psychometric evidence;
4. reliability and floor/ceiling behavior in the target population;
5. responsiveness to intervention-related change;
6. informant source and susceptibility to expectancy;
7. overlap with core ADHD symptom content;
8. licensing, cost, administration burden and scoring constraints;
9. feasibility of teacher or blinded-independent corroboration.

## Emotion Regulation Checklist

Current status: deprioritized as the automatic primary endpoint.

Strengths:

- direct historical focus on child emotion regulation and lability;
- caregiver or observer report;
- a Polish translation is reported as available in secondary sources.

Concerns:

- the 2021 evidence-base update by Mazefsky et al. identified important limitations in the original psychometric evidence and did not recommend the ERC as a preferred general emotion-regulation questionnaire;
- factor structure and construct coverage have been questioned in later work;
- availability of a translation is not the same as an independently validated Polish norm or demonstrated responsiveness to change in Polish children with ADHD.

Required before reconsideration: locate the exact Polish adaptation, validation sample, scoring permissions, norms if applicable, and intervention responsiveness evidence.

## Emotion Regulation Index for Children and Adolescents

Current status: scientifically plausible candidate, but Polish implementation evidence is currently insufficient.

Strengths:

- developed specifically around emotion-regulation processes;
- original self-report age range overlaps the planned ARC-ADHD population;
- published work in youth with ADHD has used ERICA-based measures;
- the evidence-base update by Mazefsky et al. judged ERICA to have better psychometric support than ERC.

Concerns:

- the original ERICA validation is commonly reported for ages 9–16, which leaves the planned age of 8 outside that original validation range;
- focused searches performed for this audit did not identify a validated Polish ERICA suitable for immediate use;
- self-report alone would not solve expectancy or common-method concerns;
- a parent-report adaptation has been used in ADHD research, but it is an adaptation of the original self-report and its authorization, independent validation and Polish-language status must be established separately.

Required before selection: resolve the lower-age boundary from the authoritative original instrument source, identify an authorized Polish version or perform a formal translation/adaptation pathway, verify age-specific psychometrics in Polish children, and establish responsiveness to intervention-related change.

## Emotion Dysregulation Inventory

Current status: **leading provisional Phase 1/2 endpoint candidate**, but not ready to freeze as the confirmatory primary endpoint because the Polish-language evidence package is incomplete.

Strengths:

- designed as an informant measure of observable emotion dysregulation and explicitly developed for sensitivity to change;
- validated with item-response-theory methods;
- the original validation demonstrated test-retest stability and sensitivity to change;
- a subsequent nationally representative youth study broadened evidence beyond the original autism-focused calibration work;
- the 2021 evidence-base review rated the EDI among the strongest available child/adolescent emotion-dysregulation measures;
- current instrument documentation describes the EDI as suitable from age 6 upward, including general-community and clinical samples;
- a 2026 JAACAP study links the EDI (6–17 years) and EDI-YC (2–5 years) onto a common metric across general and neurodevelopmental populations, further strengthening developmental continuity;
- current Joint Commission instrument documentation lists the EDI as public domain and supports parent/guardian, teacher or direct-provider reporting;
- the target ARC-ADHD age range of 8–12 years is fully contained within the EDI age range.

Concerns:

- the original calibration emphasized autism and neurodevelopmental populations, so measurement behavior in Polish children with ADHD still requires direct justification despite later general-population evidence;
- focused searches performed for this audit did not identify an indexed independent Polish EDI validation for children aged 8–12 years; this is evidence of an unresolved search result, not proof that no Polish work exists;
- public-domain status reduces the general instrument-access barrier but does not by itself establish a validated Polish translation or authorize an ad hoc translation to be treated as psychometrically equivalent;
- the exact ARC-ADHD endpoint still needs a frozen form/score, most plausibly the informant Reactivity score, with full versus short-form choice decided before recruitment;
- Polish responsiveness to change remains unverified.

Required before confirmatory freeze: establish the Polish-language translation/adaptation and validation pathway, archive the exact version and scoring method, confirm the intended informant, document the form/score selected, and justify longitudinal interpretation in the target ADHD population.

**Provisional Phase 1/2 decision:** use EDI Reactivity as the leading external emotion-dysregulation endpoint candidate for protocol development and power/planning work. This designation does not authorize inferential use of an unvalidated Polish translation and does not freeze the future confirmatory primary endpoint.

Evidence checked:

- Mazefsky et al. (2018), DOI 10.1002/aur.1947;
- Mazefsky et al. (2021), DOI 10.1080/15374416.2019.1703710;
- evidence-base review: Mazefsky et al. (2021), DOI 10.1080/15374416.2021.1955372;

## BRIEF2 Emotion Regulation Index

Current status: plausible secondary or supporting measure; primary status unresolved.

Strengths:

- clinically used executive-function framework;
- parent and teacher forms;
- includes an Emotion Regulation Index;
- the official PAR translation/licensing page lists Polish among available BRIEF2 Parent Form translations;
- published ADHD samples support clinical utility of BRIEF2 profiles.

Concerns:

- the ERI is embedded within a broader executive-function instrument;
- construct overlap with the intervention's executive-control targets can make an apparent far-transfer effect less independent;
- publisher-listed translation availability does not establish independent Polish validation or sensitivity to treatment-related change.

Required before selection: confirm independent Polish psychometric documentation, current licensing conditions, available informants, and whether the ERI has adequate responsiveness for an intervention endpoint. Official translation availability alone is not sufficient evidence of Polish validation.

## CBCL dysregulation profile

Current status: useful broad dysregulation or secondary measure; primary status unresolved.

Strengths:

- Polish is listed among ASEBA translations, and recent Polish child research explicitly reports use of the Polish CBCL/6–18;
- broad caregiver-rated behavioral and emotional coverage;
- dysregulation profiles have been studied in ADHD.

Concerns:

- CBCL-DP is deliberately transdiagnostic and combines attention, aggression and anxious/depressed content;
- substantial overlap with broad psychopathology and ADHD-related behavior can make it too nonspecific for the proposed regulation mechanism;
- a profile designed for clinical characterization is not automatically optimal as a change-sensitive trial endpoint.

Required before selection: define the exact dimensional score, establish licensing/scoring rules, verify the applicable Polish norms and assess responsiveness.

## Current shortlist and decision rule

The current hierarchy is:

1. **EDI Reactivity**, leading provisional Phase 1/2 endpoint candidate, conditional on a defensible Polish adaptation/validation pathway;
2. **ERICA**, scientifically relevant but currently weaker for ARC-ADHD because Polish evidence is unresolved and the original validation-age boundary may exclude 8-year-olds;
3. **BRIEF2 ERI**, useful supporting/secondary candidate because a Polish Parent Form translation is officially listed, but construct overlap and responsiveness remain concerns;
4. **CBCL-derived dysregulation**, broad external corroborating/secondary candidate rather than a narrow regulation endpoint;
5. **ERC**, retained only as a historical comparator and not the preferred default.

This ordering is a development decision, not a confirmatory ranking derived from participant outcomes.

The future confirmatory primary endpoint remains formally unfrozen. A provisional EDI decision can guide Phase 1/2 protocol development, instrument-localization work and sample-planning assumptions, but confirmatory lock requires the complete evidence package below.

## Required evidence package before freeze

For each finalist, archive:

- exact instrument/version;
- copyright/licensing status;
- Polish translation source;
- Polish validation citation and sample;
- age coverage;
- scoring and interpretation;
- reliability in the relevant age group;
- evidence of longitudinal responsiveness;
- expected floor/ceiling behavior;
- respondent burden;
- overlap with ADHD symptom content;
- whether teacher or independent corroboration is available.

The future measurement package should include at least one independent source where feasible, such as teacher report or blinded behavioral assessment, because the caregiver who observes intervention use cannot be assumed to be blinded.

## Current web/source verification notes

Verification date: 2026-09-28.

- EDI is currently documented for age 6+ and as usable in general-community and clinical samples; the 2026 common-metric paper explicitly describes EDI ages 6–17 and links it to EDI-YC ages 2–5.
- Current Joint Commission instrument documentation identifies EDI as public domain and allows parent/guardian, teacher and direct-provider reporters.
- The original EDI psychometric work demonstrated sensitivity to change, but this does not establish Polish responsiveness in ADHD.
- Focused searches on 2026-09-28 did not identify an indexed independent Polish EDI or ERICA validation suitable to treat either instrument as immediately validated for Polish children aged 8–12 years.
- ERICA has published use in ADHD research, including a parent-report adaptation, but the original measure is self-report and the original validation is commonly reported for ages 9–16.
- PAR's official translation/licensing listing includes Polish for the BRIEF2 Parent Form. This verifies translation availability, not an independent Polish validation study.
- ASEBA documentation supports CBCL/6–18 age coverage and professional administration; Polish translation/use evidence remains stronger than for EDI/ERICA, but the dysregulation profile is broader and less mechanism-specific.
- Polish DERS-8 and ERQ-S psychometric studies were identified, but the retrieved Polish validation samples are adults and therefore do not solve the 8–12-year-old endpoint problem.
- The Polish Self-Regulation Scale has adolescent validation, but it measures broader cognitive, behavioral and emotional self-regulation and is not automatically interchangeable with an emotion-dysregulation endpoint.

**Audit decision:** EDI Reactivity is the provisional external endpoint candidate for Phase 1/2 planning. No confirmatory endpoint is frozen by this audit update.
